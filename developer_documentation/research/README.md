# Veritate research papers

Plain-language write-ups of the Veritate research programme: an effort to build a small, fast, byte-level language model that both trains and runs on a single consumer machine (one Apple M3 Ultra), at zero marginal cost. "Byte-level" means the model reads and writes raw text one byte at a time, drawing on only 256 possible values, with no word-piece dictionary and no tokenizer to train, tune, or ship. The whole programme is organized around one constraint taken as a design target rather than an excuse: a single desktop, no cloud, no recurring bill, and the demand that the result still be genuinely useful.

That constraint is not incidental. Almost every published advance in language modelling assumes a datacentre: thousands of accelerators, sparse-routing kernels, fused attention, and training budgets measured in trillions of tokens. The interesting question is which of those advances survive when the accelerator count drops to one, the kernels are whatever the consumer framework happens to provide, and the token budget is bounded by a few days of a desktop GPU. Some survive intact, some invert, and some fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the idea and everything to do with the hardware. These papers are the record of finding out which is which, one measured experiment at a time.

Each paper is written twice over, in a single document. A non-engineer should be able to follow the abstract and the "what it means" section and come away with an accurate picture of what was done and what it changes. An experienced machine-learning reader should be able to assess the method itself from "what was done" and "what was measured", down to the arm definitions, the falsifiers, and the noise bands. Terms are defined at first use, jargon is translated rather than assumed, and the plain-language and technical readings are meant to agree with each other rather than tell two different stories.

## Evidence discipline

Every number in these papers traces to a measured result recorded in this repository: the research ledgers (`successes.md` and `failures.md`), the campaign and analysis notes alongside them, the dated running log (`worklog.md`), and the smoke artifacts and training runs those cite. Nothing is extrapolated, rounded to flatter, or invented. Where a claim rests on a single training seed, or where a margin sits inside the noise band, the paper says so in the same breath as the result. Where an earlier reading of the data turned out to be wrong, the correction is reported rather than quietly buried, because a corrected finding is itself a result.

The honest ceiling is stated throughout, not tucked into a footnote: one M3 Ultra will not produce a frontier-scale model, and no paper claims otherwise. What the programme does claim is narrower and more useful: that specific, measurable efficiency can be bought back with the right optimizer, the right architecture, and the right training discipline, and that the size of each gain is exactly what the ledger says it is. A pre-registered falsifier accompanies most experiments: a threshold decided before the run that, if not cleared, kills the idea. Reporting the kill line, and honouring it, is treated as part of the result.

## The papers

- [Why long conversations get expensive, and what a constant-state model changes](https://carpathian.ai/publications/why-long-conversations-get-expensive): the primer the other papers assume. What attention is for, what a key-value cache holds and why a transformer cannot avoid keeping one, and what that store costs as an exchange lengthens: 64 KB per byte of context at the 270M model's shape, passing the size of the model itself after about two pages of text. Then the alternative, a fixed-size recurrent state measured at 28 MB no matter how long the conversation runs, and the price it charges, which is a forgetting curve this programme measured rather than argued around. No new experiment; the mechanism and the measured consequences gathered in one place. Start here if the phrase "the cache grows with every byte" does not already mean something specific.

- [The composed efficiency stack](https://carpathian.ai/publications/composed-efficiency-stack): four training-efficiency levers, each isolated as a single change on a shared 10M-parameter byte-level rig, then composed. Muon reaches the baseline's quality in 1.60x fewer bytes; boundary patching in 1.82x less wall-clock; constant-state recurrence lands at parity while buying a memory that does not grow with conversation length; and a hybrid trunk that fuses the last two beats both of its parents at 0.9707 bits per byte, a 1.70x wall-clock saving. Stacked, the levers are worth roughly three times the training efficiency of the starting point. This is the first instalment of the answer to a documented compute wall.

- [Negative results, and why they are worth keeping](https://carpathian.ai/publications/negative-results): the approaches that failed at 10M to 121M, and what each one taught. Fast-weight neural memory that stored soft impressions but never retrievable facts; a "think longer at test time" loop that did not think longer; byte-level selective loss that skipped exactly the bytes it should have kept; an error-correcting delta-rule memory that would not train without overflowing 32-bit arithmetic (with the full numerical forensics); a hand-designed pinned memory register that cost quality without buying recall; and the mixture-of-experts throughput tax that this specific machine, not the idea, imposes. Every kill carries a retry condition that says exactly what would reopen the question.

- [How a 121M byte model learned to talk](https://carpathian.ai/publications/learning-to-talk): the three-phase recipe (pretrain, midtrain anneal, supervised fine-tune) that made chat80m converse, the validation trajectory across the phases, the knowledge wall measured in the same smoke that confirmed the conversation, and the identity-dosing discovery, in which two opposite failure modes (drilling a fact too hard, and varying it so much it never anchors) bracket a single, stated, confirmed rule for teaching a small model one fact about itself.

- [Serving a research architecture on a CPU](https://carpathian.ai/publications/cpu-serving-engine): the v13 engine format that runs the hybrid trunk at about one millisecond per byte on a single CPU core, with a constant-size decode state, letter-for-letter parity to the reference implementation, and backwards compatibility with every earlier model format guaranteed by the design rather than hoped for. Includes the three-layer exactness discipline that makes the fast path trustworthy and two pre-existing bugs it surfaced and fixed.

- [Long-context memory for constant-state models](https://carpathian.ai/publications/long-context-memory): the conversation-needle benchmark, the finding that a small model can be taught to retrieve facts that look like its training data but does not transfer that skill to unfamiliar surface forms, the bitwise-exact streaming state carry that makes past-window recall measurable at all, and the open programme for training past-window retrieval. The candid current position: the benchmark, the finding, and the infrastructure are done; no mechanism has yet flattened the forgetting curve.

- [Amortizing the optimizer step](https://carpathian.ai/publications/amortizing-the-optimizer-step): the affine cost model of the training step (a fixed per-update overhead plus a per-token compute rate), measured on the real 270M model before a multi-day launch rather than extrapolated after, why the chunk knob is the cheapest throughput on offer, why the fastest available setting was deliberately not chosen, and the token-matched check showing the resulting roughly 2x speedup cost no quality per unit of text read.

## How the papers connect

The seven are one continuous line of work, not seven independent notes. [Why long conversations get expensive](https://carpathian.ai/publications/why-long-conversations-get-expensive) explains the mechanism the rest of them take for granted, and is the one paper that reports no experiment of its own; [The composed efficiency stack](https://carpathian.ai/publications/composed-efficiency-stack) selects the architecture; [How a 121M byte model learned to talk](https://carpathian.ai/publications/learning-to-talk) scales it into the first conversational model; [Serving a research architecture on a CPU](https://carpathian.ai/publications/cpu-serving-engine) makes that model serve on ordinary hardware; [Negative results, and why they are worth keeping](https://carpathian.ai/publications/negative-results) and [Long-context memory for constant-state models](https://carpathian.ai/publications/long-context-memory) map the boundaries of what the architecture can and cannot yet do; and [Amortizing the optimizer step](https://carpathian.ai/publications/amortizing-the-optimizer-step) tunes the machine that trains the next size up. Read in order they tell a single story: understand what remembering a conversation costs, pick the most efficient trunk that fits one desktop, teach it to talk, serve it cheaply, chart its memory limits honestly, and drive the training loop as close to its hardware ceiling as the arithmetic allows.

## Scope note

[The composed efficiency stack](https://carpathian.ai/publications/composed-efficiency-stack), [Negative results, and why they are worth keeping](https://carpathian.ai/publications/negative-results), and [Long-context memory for constant-state models](https://carpathian.ai/publications/long-context-memory) draw on the 10M-parameter architecture race (canonical shape, byte-level, the FineWeb-Edu corpus, 12000 steps per arm, one seed, single-change arms). [How a 121M byte model learned to talk](https://carpathian.ai/publications/learning-to-talk), [Serving a research architecture on a CPU](https://carpathian.ai/publications/cpu-serving-engine), and the long-context-memory work draw on chat80m, the first scaled model (a 121.75M-parameter hybrid trunk). [Amortizing the optimizer step](https://carpathian.ai/publications/amortizing-the-optimizer-step) draws on chat200m, the 270M-parameter scale-up whose pretrain launched 2026-07-08. [Why long conversations get expensive](https://carpathian.ai/publications/why-long-conversations-get-expensive) runs no rig of its own: every figure in it is quoted from one of the others or is arithmetic derived from a published model shape, and it is sourced line by line in its evidence trail.
